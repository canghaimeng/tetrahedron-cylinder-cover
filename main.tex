\begin{abstract}
We give an explicit rational parameter choice in the compatible-secant
construction of van de Kamp, itself based on OpenAI's angular cylinder
construction. For the closed regular tetrahedron of edge length two,
20,000 infinite cylinders with compact nondegenerate triangular perpendicular
bases have total base area, normalized by the minimum projection area,
strictly less than \(0.4999213\). This improves the checked public
20,000-cylinder benchmark \(0.499921585997001774\).
The parameter changes from \(e=1/10\) to \(e=13/125\); the geometric
family and its padding rule are retained. We reproduce the closed-set
coverage proof on its full domain \(0<e<1\), and certify the finite area
directly rather than extending error estimates restricted to \(e\le1/10\).
Integer square roots and an independent rational Taylor--power-sum
calculation both separate the new area from the old actual area.
The contribution is a quantitative specialization with reproducible
certificates; no optimality or exhaustive priority claim is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement, model, and comparison}
All geometry is Euclidean in \(\R^3\). Put \(H=\sqrt2\) and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K}
 K=\{(x,y,Hs):0\le s\le1,\ |x|\le1-s,\ |y|\le s\}.
\end{equation}
This is the closed regular tetrahedron with vertices
\((\pm1,0,0)\) and \((0,\pm1,H)\).
For a nonzero vector \(v\), a cylinder means \(B+\R v\),
where \(B\subset v^\perp\) is a compact nondegenerate triangle.
Let \(\pi_E\) denote orthogonal projection onto a plane \(E\), and define
\[
 \Amin(K)=\min_{\dim E=2}|\pi_E K|,\qquad
 R=\frac{\sum_j |B_j|}{\Amin(K)}.
\]
The sum counts each base separately, including overlaps. There is no
restriction on the axes' tilt angles. The denominator is one common
minimum projection area, not a different projection area for each cylinder.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The tetrahedron \(K\) admits a cover by a labeled family of \(20{,}000\)
cylinders of the above kind. With the construction below at
\(e=13/125\) and \(N=10{,}000\), its normalized total area satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newinterval}
 \frac{4999212211665836198151146}{10^{25}}
 \le R <
 \frac{4999212211665836198161146}{10^{25}}
 <\frac{4999213}{10^7}.
\end{equation}
Every point of \(K\), including its boundary, is covered.
For the published \(e=1/10,\ N=10{,}000\) instance of the same family,
with actual normalized area \(R_{\mathrm{old}}\), one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strict}
 R<\frac{4999213}{10^7}<\frac{4999215}{10^7}<R_{\mathrm{old}}.
\end{equation}
Consequently \(R<0.499921585997001774\).
\end{theorem}

OpenAI's angular construction proves a finite triangular-cylinder
cover below \(1/2\), with the same tetrahedron and normalization
\cite{openai-angular}. Its displayed theorem uses
\(m=2\lceil2/\varepsilon^2\rceil\), \(0<\varepsilon\le1/2000\), and
\[
 R_{\mathrm{OAI}}=\frac12-\frac{13}{6000}\varepsilon^2+E,\qquad
 |E|\le2\varepsilon^4.
\]
At \(\varepsilon=1/2000\), this is \(16{,}000{,}000\) cylinders.
The stated remainder implies
\[
 R_{\mathrm{OAI}}\ge
 \frac12-\frac{13}{6000\cdot2000^2}-\frac{2}{2000^4}>0.4999215.
\]
Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:main} improves that explicit realization in both
count and actual area. This comparison is with the displayed source
instance; it does not bound every possible instance of all four
OpenAI constructions \cite{openai-angular,openai-ruled,openai-slope,openai-radial}.

Van de Kamp's quartic overlap estimate and finite secant padding yield
the much stronger \(20{,}000\)-cylinder benchmark
\cite{vdk-secant,vdk-result}. The published outward interval is
\[
 [0.499921585997001674,\,0.499921585997001774].
\]
We retain that construction and prove a strict comparison with its
\emph{actual} finite area. A narrower enclosure of the same number would
not establish \eqref{eq:strict}.

\section{Normalization and explicit cylinders}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:normalization}
The tetrahedron in \eqref{eq:K} has \(\Amin(K)=H\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Its four outward face area vectors are \((0,\pm H,-1)\) and
\((\pm H,0,1)\).
For a unit vector \(u\), the forward faces project onto the shadow with
disjoint interiors, by assigning each line parallel to \(u\) its forward
endpoint. The area of a projected face is the absolute scalar product
of its area vector with \(u\). The four area vectors sum to zero,
so the shadow area is half the sum of their four absolute products:
\[
 A(u)=\max(H|u_x|,|u_z|)+\max(H|u_y|,|u_z|).
\]
Let \(U=\max(|u_x|,|u_z|/H)\) and \(V=\max(|u_y|,|u_z|/H)\).
Then \(U^2\ge u_x^2\), \(V^2\ge u_y^2\), and
\(2UV\ge2u_z^2/H^2=u_z^2\).
Thus \((U+V)^2\ge1\), so \(A(u)\ge H\).
The direction \(u=(1,0,0)\) gives equality.
\end{proof}

This is the equal-partition case of \cite[Sections 1--3]{vdk-secant}.
We reproduce its proof to make the parameter domain explicit.
Take any \(0<e<1\) and integer \(N\ge1\). For \(0\le i<N\), define
\begin{gather}
 a=\frac1N,\quad d=2a,\quad l_i=-1+\frac{2i}{N},\quad
 r_i=l_i+d,\quad c_i=\frac{l_i+r_i}{2},\quad I_i=[l_i,r_i],\label{eq:grid}\\
 G(q)=\frac{1+q^2}{2},\quad D(q)=\frac{1-q^2}{2},\quad
 C(q)=1+e^2q^2G(q)+\frac9{16}e^4,\label{eq:cap}\\
 A_i=\frac{1+l_i r_i}{2}=G(c_i)-\frac{a^2}{2},\qquad
 \Mstar=1+e^2+\frac9{16}e^4,\\
 K_0=\Mstar\left(e+\frac{e^2}{2}\right)+2e^2,\qquad
 \delta=\frac{K_0a+\Mstar e^2a^2/2}{1-e},\label{eq:padding}\\
 M_i=C\bigl(\max(|l_i|,|r_i|)\bigr)+\delta.\label{eq:Mi}
\end{gather}
Since \(C\) is even and increasing on \([0,1]\),
\(M_i=\max_{q\in I_i}C(q)+\delta>0\).
Use the intercept triangles
\begin{align}
 P_i&=\{(0,qt,Ht):q\in I_i,\ 0\le t\le M_i/2\},\\
 Q_i&=\{(pt,0,H(1-t)):p\in I_i,\ 0\le t\le M_i/2\},
\end{align}
and the axes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:axes}
 v_i=(1,eA_i,Hec_i),\qquad w_i=(-eA_i,1,Hec_i).
\end{equation}
The cylinders are \(P_i+\R v_i\) and \(Q_i+\R w_i\), with bases
\(B_i^-=\pi_{v_i^\perp}P_i\), \(B_i^+=\pi_{w_i^\perp}Q_i\).
For example \(P_i\) is the convex hull of
\((0,0,0)\), \((0,l_iM_i/2,HM_i/2)\), and
\((0,r_iM_i/2,HM_i/2)\).
It is a compact nondegenerate triangle because \(dM_i>0\).
Orthogonal projection is injective on its intercept plane:
an axis-parallel vector in \(x=0\) must vanish, as the first coordinate
of \(v_i\) is one. The analogous argument uses \(y=0\) and \(w_i\).
Both projected triangles remain nondegenerate.
Projection removes an axis-parallel component, so it preserves the cylinder.

\section{Coverage of the entire closed tetrahedron}
\subsection{Angular selection and exact membership}
Fix \(z=(x,y,Hs)\in K\). Its axis-parallel intercept in \(x=0\) is
\[
 z-xv_i=(0,y_0,Ht_0),\qquad y_0=y-exA_i,\quad t_0=s-exc_i.
\]
Since the first coordinate of \(v_i\) is one, this intercept is unique.
Thus, including when \(t_0=0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:membership}
 z\in P_i+\R v_i\ \Longleftrightarrow\
 0\le t_0\le M_i/2,\quad l_it_0\le y_0\le r_it_0.
\end{equation}
Likewise \(z-yw_j=(x_0,0,H(1-s_0))\), where
\(s_0=1-s+eyc_j\), \(x_0=x+eyA_j\), so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uppermembership}
 z\in Q_j+\R w_j\ \Longleftrightarrow\
 0\le s_0\le M_j/2,\quad l_js_0\le x_0\le r_js_0.
\end{equation}
Let \(q_k=-1+2k/N\), \(0\le k\le N\), and
\(F_k=sq_k+exD(q_k)\).
Since \(D(-1)=D(1)=0\), we have \(F_0=-s\le y\le s=F_N\).
Choose the first \(k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\) with \(F_k\ge y\).
Then \(F_{k-1}\le y\), including when \(y=F_0\).
Set \(i=k-1\). The identities
\[
 A_i-l_ic_i=D(l_i),\qquad A_i-r_ic_i=D(r_i)
\]
convert \(F_{k-1}\le y\le F_k\) to \(l_it_0\le y_0\le r_it_0\).
As \(r_i-l_i>0\), these inequalities imply \(t_0\ge0\).
No monotonicity of \(F_k\) is needed.

Apply the same argument to \(E_k=(1-s)q_k-eyD(q_k)\).
Its endpoints bracket \(x\), since \(|x|\le1-s\). We obtain a cell
\(j\) with \(s_0\ge0\) and \(l_js_0\le x_0\le r_js_0\).
If \(t_0=0\), then \(y_0=0\) and \eqref{eq:membership} gives coverage.
If \(s_0=0\), then \(x_0=0\) and \eqref{eq:uppermembership} does so.
Assume both are positive before dividing.

\subsection{A weighted identity}
Set
\[
 \rho=2t_0,\quad \sigma=2s_0,\quad q=y_0/t_0\in I_i,\quad
 p=x_0/s_0\in I_j,\quad X=2x,\quad Y=2y.
\]
The intercept equations become
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intercepts}
 Y=\rho q+eXA_i,\quad X=\sigma p-eYA_j,\quad
 \rho+\sigma=2+e(Yc_j-Xc_i).
\end{equation}
Let \(\Lambda=1+e^2A_iA_j\).
The first two equations give
\(\Lambda X=\sigma p-eA_j\rho q\) and
\(\Lambda Y=\rho q+eA_i\sigma p\).
Substitution in the third yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted}
 w_\rho\rho+w_\sigma\sigma=2\Lambda,
\end{equation}
where
\[
 w_\rho=1-eqc_j+e^2A_j(A_i-qc_i),\qquad
 w_\sigma=1+epc_i+e^2A_i(A_j-pc_j).
\]
The affine function \(L_i(q)=A_i-qc_i\) interpolates
\(D(l_i),D(r_i)\ge0\), and \(0\le A_i,A_j\le1\).
All angular variables and centers have absolute value at most one.
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive}
 w_\rho,w_\sigma\ge1-e>0.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Continuous overlap and finite padding}
For \(p,q\in[-1,1]\), write
\[
 N_0=1+e^2G(p)G(q),\quad A_-=1-epq+e^2G(p)D(q),\quad
 B_+=1+epq+e^2G(q)D(p).
\]
The overlap certificate of \cite{vdk-secant} is
\begin{align}
 &A_-C(q)+B_+C(p)-2N_0 \nonumber\\
 &\quad=\frac{e^2}{2}
 \left(p^2-q^2+\frac{epq(1+p^2+q^2)}2\right)^2 \nonumber\\
 &\qquad+e^4\left[
 G(p)G(q)\{p^2D(p)+q^2D(q)\}
 +\frac{9-p^2q^2(1+p^2+q^2)^2}{8}\right]\label{eq:overlap}\\
 &\qquad+\frac{9e^6}{16}\{G(p)D(q)+G(q)D(p)\}.\nonumber
\end{align}
To verify it directly, put \(\Delta=p^2-q^2\), \(S=1+p^2+q^2\).
Multiplication of the left side gives
\begin{align*}
 &\frac{e^2\Delta^2}{2}+\frac{e^3pq\Delta S}{2}\\
 &\quad+e^4\left[\frac98+G(p)G(q)\{p^2D(p)+q^2D(q)\}\right]
 +\frac{9e^6}{16}\{G(p)D(q)+G(q)D(p)\}.
\end{align*}
Completing the square proves \eqref{eq:overlap}.
All terms on its right are nonnegative:
\(G,D\ge0\), \(p^2q^2\le1\), and \(S\le3\).

For the secant perturbation, put \(b=q-c_i\), so \(|b|\le a\). Then
\[
 L_i(q)=D(q)-E_i,\quad E_i=\frac{a^2-b^2}{2},\quad
 0\le E_i\le\frac{a^2}{2},\quad
 |A_i-G(q)|\le|c_i|a+a^2\le a.
\]
The last inequality uses \(|c_i|+a\le1\). The corresponding statements
hold for \(p\in I_j\).
Using \(D\le1/2\) and \(G,A_i,A_j\le1\), we get
\begin{align}
 |w_\rho-A_-|,\ |w_\sigma-B_+|&\le ea+\frac{e^2}{2}(a+a^2),\label{eq:weightloss}\\
 |\Lambda-N_0|&\le2e^2a.\label{eq:detloss}
\end{align}
For example the quadratic part of the first bound follows from
\[
 A_jL_i(q)-G(p)D(q)=(A_j-G(p))D(q)-A_jE_i,
\]
and its linear part is at most \(e|q||p-c_j|\le ea\).
Changing the two factors \(A_i,A_j\) one at a time proves
\eqref{eq:detloss}.
Since \(0<C\le\Mstar\), equations
\eqref{eq:overlap}--\eqref{eq:detloss} imply
\[
 w_\rho C(q)+w_\sigma C(p)-2\Lambda\ge-2K_0a-\Mstar e^2a^2.
\]
But the definition of \(\delta\) and \eqref{eq:positive} give
\[
 (w_\rho+w_\sigma)\delta\ge2(1-e)\delta=2K_0a+\Mstar e^2a^2.
\]
As \(M_i\ge C(q)+\delta\) and \(M_j\ge C(p)+\delta\), it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capbudget}
 w_\rho M_i+w_\sigma M_j\ge2\Lambda.
\end{equation}
If both cylinders missed \(z\), their angular membership conditions
would already hold, forcing \(\rho>M_i\) and \(\sigma>M_j\).
The positive weights would contradict \eqref{eq:weighted}
and \eqref{eq:capbudget}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:coverage}
For every \(0<e<1\) and integer \(N\ge1\), these \(2N\) cylinders cover
all of the closed tetrahedron \(K\).
\end{proposition}
All selection and membership inequalities were closed, with zero radii
handled before division. Thus the proof includes vertices, faces, edges,
grid nodes, and the interface between families. No sampling is used.

\section{Area and exact arithmetic certificates}
Each intercept triangle has physical area \(HdM_i^2/8\).
Projection multiplies area by the absolute scalar product of the
intercept-plane and projection-plane unit normals, namely
\(1/\sqrt{Z_i}\), where
\[
 Z_i=|v_i|^2=|w_i|^2=1+e^2(A_i^2+2c_i^2).
\]
The two families contribute equally. Lemma~\ref{lem:normalization} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:area}
 R(e,N)=\frac1{2N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{M_i^2}{\sqrt{Z_i}}.
\end{equation}
This exact identity holds for \(0<e<1\). It does not use the source's
later integral approximation, stated only for \(e\le1/10\).

\subsection{Integer square-root enclosures}
Take \(N=10{,}000\), \(e=13/125\). The padding is exactly
\[
 \delta=\frac{161414920845251281}{10937500000000000000000}.
\]
Let \(S=10^{25}\), \(b_i=M_i^2/(2N)\), and express
\(b_i^2/Z_i=u_i/v_i\) as a positive rational number. Define
\[
 k_i=\isqrt\left(\left\lfloor S^2u_i/v_i\right\rfloor\right).
\]
Here \(\isqrt(n)\) is the greatest nonnegative integer whose square is
at most \(n\). The integer inequalities
\[
 k_i^2v_i\le S^2u_i<(k_i+1)^2v_i
\]
give \(k_i/S\le b_i/\sqrt{Z_i}<(k_i+1)/S\).
The implementation checks them for every cell using exact integers.
It obtains
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}crr@{}}
\toprule
\(e\) & \(\sum_i k_i\) & \(\sum_i(k_i+1)\)\\
\midrule
\(13/125\) & \(4999212211665836198151146\) & \(4999212211665836198161146\)\\
\(1/10\) & \(4999215859970016769600378\) & \(4999215859970016769610378\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The first row proves \eqref{eq:newinterval}; the second proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oldinterval}
 \frac{4999215859970016769600378}{10^{25}}\le R_{\mathrm{old}}<
 \frac{4999215859970016769610378}{10^{25}}.
\end{equation}
Comparison \eqref{eq:strict} now follows by integer arithmetic.
The generator and certificate are \path{code/retuned_secants.py}
and \path{checkers/retuned_certificate.json}.

\subsection{An independent Taylor--power-sum check}
A second implementation does not import the constructor, use integer
square roots, or read the first certificate.
Reflection sends cell \(i\) to \(N-1-i\), negating \(c_i\) and preserving
\(A_i,M_i,Z_i\). Since \(N=10{,}000\) is even and zero is a node, exactly
half the cells are positive. For \(1\le k\le5000\), put
\begin{gather*}
 q=\frac{k}{5000},\quad c=\frac{2k-1}{10000},\quad
 A=\frac12\left(1+\frac{k(k-1)}{25000000}\right),\\
 M=1+\frac{e^2}{2}(q^2+q^4)+\frac9{16}e^4+\delta,\qquad
 z=e^2(A^2+2c^2).
\end{gather*}
Thus \(R(e,10000)=\frac1{10000}\sum_{k=1}^{5000}M^2(1+z)^{-1/2}\).
Define
\[
 T_4(z)=1-\frac z2+\frac{3z^2}{8}-\frac{5z^3}{16}+\frac{35z^4}{128},
 \qquad T_5(z)=T_4(z)-\frac{63z^5}{256}.
\]
The \(j\)-th derivative of \((1+z)^{-1/2}\) has sign \((-1)^j\) on
\([0,\infty)\). Taylor's theorem with remainder gives
\(T_5(z)\le(1+z)^{-1/2}\le T_4(z)\) for \(z\ge0\).
The weighted sums are polynomials in \(k\) with rational coefficients.

For \(S_j(m)=\sum_{k=1}^m k^j\), telescoping
\((k+1)^{j+1}-k^{j+1}\) gives
\[
 S_0(m)=m,\qquad
 S_j(m)=\frac{(m+1)^{j+1}-1-
 \sum_{h=0}^{j-1}\binom{j+1}{h}S_h(m)}{j+1}.
\]
Exact polynomial multiplication and this recurrence produce
\begin{align*}
 0.499921221156277387&\le R(13/125,10000)\le0.499921221578204204,\\
 0.499921585990560669&\le R(1/10,10000)\le0.499921586275233398.
\end{align*}
These are outward-rounded decimal intervals; they independently prove
\eqref{eq:strict}. The unrounded fractions are in
\path{checkers/power_sum_bounds.json}; the implementation is
\path{checkers/check_retuned.py}.
Either certificate, together with Proposition~\ref{prop:coverage} and
the nondegeneracy argument, proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Reproducibility and proof dependencies}\label{sec:reproduce}
The package uses Python 3.8 or later and only its standard library;
the recorded runs used Python 3.12.3. No random seed, floating-point
arithmetic, network connection, or stored cylinder list is needed.
From the directory containing this paper, run:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 code/reproduce.py
python3 code/retuned_secants.py --index 0
python3 code/retuned_secants.py --index 19999
latexmk -pdf -jobname=paper -interaction=nonstopmode main.tex
\end{verbatim}
The first command recomputes both certificates, checks the archived
outputs, and checks the overlap identity with the separate reviewer's
exact polynomial implementation. It also checks the rational comparison
with the displayed OpenAI instance. Failure gives a nonzero exit code.
These computations complement the universal geometric proof.

The indexed generator returns rational data in coordinates \((x,y,z/H)\),
whose physical metric is \(\operatorname{diag}(1,1,2)\).
A perpendicular vertex is computed by
\(P\mapsto P-\langle P,v\rangle v/\langle v,v\rangle\).
Indices \(0,\ldots,9999\) designate the lower family;
\(10000,\ldots,19999\) designate the upper family.
These formulas specify all triangles without a large coordinate file.

For a later formalization, the dependency order is:
projection minimum; triangle construction and injective projection;
exact membership and first crossing; positive weighted-radius identity;
continuous polynomial identity; secant perturbation and padding;
closed-body coverage; area formula; rational certificates; strict comparison.
All hypotheses are \(N\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}\), \(0<e<1\), and the displayed
definitions, specialized to \(N=10000,e=13/125\) for the numerical theorem.
No Lean theorem for this new instance has been kernel checked here.
